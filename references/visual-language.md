# Visual Language System

Use this system after content planning and before template selection. Visual language is evidence strategy, not surface decoration.

## Design principles

1. **Make invisible systems tangible.** Use abstract 3D form to represent distributed intelligence, emergence, transformation, scale, and interconnected infrastructure without inventing a literal product interface.
2. **Separate atmosphere from evidence.** A mesh or fluid object establishes conceptual tone. A chart, metric, photograph, or quotation proves the claim. Never let an atmospheric asset impersonate evidence.
3. **Keep the canvas editorial.** Neutral space carries authority. Saturated color marks transitions, data, or one focal object rather than filling every available region.
4. **Use one dominant visual verb.** A page may flow, gather, expand, compare, reveal, or resolve. Do not combine several unrelated visual gestures.

## Image asset language

### Abstract 3D vocabulary

- Use **particle fields** for adoption, distributed activity, ecosystems, networks, or many agents acting together.
- Use **woven mesh surfaces** for orchestration, connected workflows, infrastructure, governance, or systems operating as one.
- Use **fluid folds** for transformation, acceleration, transition, uncertainty becoming structure, or an argument changing phase.
- Use **faceted translucent forms** for investment, value creation, layered capability, or measurable business outcomes.
- Use **real photographs** only when a real person, place, product, organization, or case artifact is itself evidence.
- Never use abstract 3D art to represent a precise product feature, interface, geography, financial instrument, or named real-world entity.

### Relationship to information

- Assign every asset one role: `atmospheric-anchor`, `evidence-carrier`, `authority-anchor`, `transition-field`, or `none`.
- Atmospheric assets must support the headline's semantic verb and remain outside the main reading path.
- Evidence images require a caption, source, or explicit case relationship.
- Portraits remain small and adjacent to attribution. They do not become hero images unless the page is explicitly person-led.
- One primary image asset per ordinary page. A multi-image emotional composition is allowed only under the E01-E07 rules.

### Image occupancy

- Ordinary analytical page: 0-28% of canvas.
- Narrative or quotation page: 18-38%.
- Chapter transition: 25-55%, normally edge-cropped or integrated into the gradient field.
- Case evidence image: 28-50% when the image proves the claim.
- Full bleed: cover, emotional transition, or closing only; maintain a protected text field.
- Crop 25-55% of abstract mesh assets beyond an edge. Never center a complete mesh object like a product render.

## Color language

### Usage

- Keep ordinary pages 70-85% neutral canvas by visible area.
- Keep ordinary-page saturated accents near 5-15%; allow up to 30% only for a justified evidence panel.
- Use full-canvas Google-adjacent gradients for chapter transitions and selected visual-climax pages.
- Use continuous gradients in chart marks when they encode progress, maturity, change, or chapter color continuity.
- Use near-black for evidence concentration, comparison, or closing—not for random alternation.
- Advance chapter hues by adjacency: warm -> yellow/green -> green/blue -> blue/violet.

### Prohibitions

- No adjacent solid rectangles pretending to be a gradient.
- No four-color rainbow decoration without data meaning.
- No gradient body text.
- No saturated background behind dense prose.
- No alarm red for ordinary risk pages.
- No more than one dominant saturated field on an ordinary page.
- Do not repeat the same accent corner, mesh crop, or gradient direction on three consecutive pages.

## Spatial language

- Preserve 25-45% meaningful empty space on ordinary pages; quotation and transition pages may preserve 45-65%.
- Place the first visual focus in the upper-left, upper-center, or evidence-side upper third. Avoid dead-center composition except a deliberate portal or single KPI.
- Maintain one primary alignment axis and at most one secondary evidence axis.
- Separate the headline zone from the evidence zone by at least one body-line height.
- Keep 24-48 design units between atmospheric assets and readable text.
- Treat low-density pages as punctuation. They must change scale, field, or visual silence—not merely contain fewer words.

## Executable decision rules

1. Write the page claim and evidence type.
2. Choose `visual_role`.
3. If the role is atmospheric, choose one abstract vocabulary from the headline verb.
4. If the role is evidence, prefer an editable chart, metric, sourced image, or quotation before abstract art.
5. Set `visual_asset_strategy` and target occupancy before layout.
6. Reject the asset if removing it leaves the page's meaning unchanged and the page already has a focal point.
7. Record external asset provenance in speaker notes.
