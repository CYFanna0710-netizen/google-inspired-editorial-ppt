# Presentation Rhythm System

Rhythm is the controlled change of cognitive load, evidence form, scale, and visual field across the deck.

## Page types

- `narrative`: establishes context, thesis, interpretation, or synthesis.
- `data`: carries a chart, matrix, or comparable metrics.
- `evidence`: proves a claim through a decisive KPI, quotation, or sourced artifact.
- `case`: connects objective, action, outcome, and authority.
- `chapter`: resets the visual field and announces a new argument phase.
- `action`: converts the argument into decisions, checklist, or CTA.

## Target bands

Use these as planning bands, not quotas. Source evidence always wins.

| Page type | Typical share in 12+ slide analytical decks |
|---|---:|
| narrative | 20-35% |
| data | 20-40% |
| evidence | 15-30% |
| case | 8-25% |
| chapter | 5-15% |
| action | 5-15% |

Do not fabricate a page type to hit a percentage. Report an imbalance when the source cannot support the desired rhythm.

## Sequence grammar

- Begin with thesis or orientation, then establish the first supported claim.
- Move from narrative -> data/evidence -> interpretation or case.
- Use a low-density page after 2-4 medium/high-density pages.
- Do not repeat one template more than twice consecutively.
- Do not repeat one page type more than three times consecutively unless the deck is a deliberate appendix or data book.
- Do not repeat one `visual_asset_strategy` more than twice consecutively.
- A chapter page must correspond to a genuine argument boundary.
- A visual climax increases scale, contrast, or evidence concentration; it is not merely a brighter background.

## Short deck contract

For decks of 10 pages or fewer, require source-supported versions of:

1. One `visual-climax` page.
2. One `strong-data` page containing a chart, comparable metrics, or decisive sourced KPI.
3. One `synthesis` or `action` page that states the point of view or next decision.

If the source lacks one of these, emit a warning and request content rather than inventing evidence.

## Rhythm positions

Use: `opening`, `orientation`, `early-argument`, `evidence-build`, `visual-climax`, `proof`, `midpoint-reset`, `synthesis`, `action`, or `closing`.

Assign positions from narrative function and deck progress. Progress may break ties, but must never determine the template or claim.

## Validation rules

- Same template on more than two consecutive pages: warning.
- Narrative/text-only run longer than three pages: warning.
- No chapter or visual-climax page: warning.
- Same visual strategy on more than two consecutive pages: warning.
- More than three high-density pages in any five-page window: warning.
- Short deck missing visual climax, strong data, or synthesis/action: warning.
- A deck dominated by one page type above 55%: warning unless explicitly classified as an appendix/data book.
